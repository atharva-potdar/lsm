# Hypothesis H5: Analytical Write-Amplification Models Overpredict Under Skew

## 1. The Hypothesis
Classical Log-Structured Merge (LSM) models estimate leveled Write Amplification (WA) as $WA = 1 + \frac{T}{2} \cdot L$. We hypothesize that under highly skewed Zipfian workloads, empirical WA will be significantly lower because hot keys deduplicate in upper tiers ($L_0 \to L_1$), preventing cascading rewrites to deeper levels. 

## 2. Experimental Setup & Disentangling Confounds
To measure steady-state overwrite absorption, we built a mathematically rigorous V3 harness:
1. **Fisher-Yates Permutation:** Maps Zipfian ranks bijectively to the key space, preserving exact frequencies while eliminating lexicographical clustering artifacts.
2. **Deep-Tree Geometry:** MemTable (512 KB), Target SST (256 KB), $L_1$ Base (4 MB), $T=10$.
3. **Restricted Uniform Control Arms:** Uniform workloads artificially restricted to the exact distinct key cardinalities generated by the skewed workloads, allowing us to isolate working-set shrinkage from pure skew deduplication.

## 4. Physical Discoveries

### Finding A: Deep-Level I/O is Strictly Linear with Distinct Keys (Invariant to Skew)
The most striking finding is that deep-level compaction ($L_2$ and $L_3$) is entirely agnostic to the shape of the access distribution. It scales linearly with the absolute number of *distinct keys touched* ($U$), regardless of whether those keys were generated by Uniform or Zipfian access.
Applying a linear fit to the deep levels across all arms:
* $L_3 \text{ Output} \approx 107.1\text{ MB} + 0.71 \cdot U$ 
* $L_2 \text{ Output} \approx 39.5\text{ MB} + 0.98 \cdot U$ 

*Conclusion:* Skew does not magically prevent data from cascading to the bottom of the tree; it simply reduces the size of the active working set, which mechanically lowers deep-level I/O.

### Finding B: Pure Skew Overwrite Absorption is Concentrated at $L_1$
By comparing the High Zipf run ($\theta=0.99$, $76.1k$ keys) against the matched Uniform Control Arm ($77k$ keys), we decompose the Write Amplification savings:
* **Footprint Shrinkage:** Comparing Uniform (3.129 WA) to Control 77k (2.905 WA) shows a $\sim 66\%$ contribution to total savings.
* **Pure Skew Deduplication:** Comparing Control 77k (2.905 WA) to High Zipf (2.791 WA) isolates the pure overwrite absorption effect, representing $\sim 34\%$ of the total savings. 
This skew-only effect is structurally constrained to the $L_0 \to L_1$ transition, where temporal locality causes hot keys to collide within a single merge window. 

### Finding C: The Quantized Cascade Effect
Per-seed forensic analysis revealed that Write Amplification in leveled LSM trees is highly non-continuous. Variance across seeds (e.g., Uniform WA ranging from $3.112$ to $3.869$) was heavily quantized. Outlier seeds jumped by exactly $\sim 40\text{ MB}$ or $\sim 107\text{ MB}$—the exact capacities of $L_2$ and $L_3$. This indicates that window-based WA measurement is easily corrupted by single, discrete full-level capacity triggers crossing the threshold just prior to window close.

### Finding D: The Classical Model vs. Empirical Best-Case
To isolate whether the classical formula ($1 + \frac{T}{2}L$) overpredicts due to overwrites or simply due to its pessimistic assumptions, we ran an `Insert-Only` workload. Because this arm generated keys sequentially without overlap, it triggered trivial pass-through compactions ($L_0 \to L_1 \to L_2 \to L_3$ moved exactly 503 MB each). Despite zero overwrites, empirical WA sat at $4.50$, while a dynamically calibrated geometry model predicted $7.35$. This proves that the analytical gap persists even under 0% deduplication.

## 5. Conclusion
Models estimating LSM write wear cannot assume a monolithic reduction factor for skewed workloads. The data explicitly decompose the mechanics: deep-level I/O ($L_2+$) must be modeled as a strict linear function of distinct working-set cardinality, while pure skew overwrite deduplication operates exclusively as an $L_1$ attenuation factor.
